# Mission format

`tmp/<slug>-mission.md` captures the *reason* the cohort is taking this
course. Every teaching decision traces back to this file.

## Template

```md
# Mission: <Course title>

## Why
1-3 sentences. The concrete real-world capability the cohort is
chasing. What changes in their life or work when they have this skill?
Avoid abstract framings like "to understand X". Push for the
underlying outcome.

## Success looks like
- A specific, observable thing a learner will be able to do at the end
- Another specific thing
- ...

## Constraints
- Time, budget, prior commitments, learning preferences, anything that
  bounds the approach.

## Out of scope
- Adjacent topics this course deliberately does not chase. Protects ZPD.
```

## Rules

- **One mission per course.** If two unrelated things, that's two
  courses.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Ship a Rust CLI to my team" beats "learn
  Rust." "Land an offer at a senior level" beats "improve interview
  skills."
- **Push back on vagueness.** If the user can't articulate why,
  interview them before writing anything. A bad mission is worse than
  no mission.
- **Revise when reality shifts.** Missions change between iterations.
  When the user's goal moves, update this file — don't leave a stale
  mission steering the build.
- **Keep it short.** If the mission runs past a screen, it has stopped
  being a compass and started being a plan.
