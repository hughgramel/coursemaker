# The title-subtitle-keyword system

---

## App name carries the most ranking weight

- The app name (title) has the highest keyword ranking weight in both stores
- App Store: 30-character limit for the name field
- Google Play: 30 characters for the title, 80 for the short description
- Formula that works: `BrandName: Primary Keyword`
  - Example: `Streaks: Habit Tracker` (not `Streaks`)
- Source: AppFollow ASO Title Playbook (2026); RevenueCat ASO guide (2022)

---

## Subtitle and keywords: the second and third layers

- App Store subtitle: 30 characters, indexed by the algorithm, shown under the title in search
- Keywords field: 100 characters, comma-separated, not visible to users
- Do not repeat words already in the title or subtitle, the algorithm ignores duplicates
- Google Play: the short description (80 chars) and full description are both indexed; there is no separate keyword field

---

## Keyword strategy: avoid where giants bid

- Apple Search Ads reveals keyword search popularity scores (0-100)
- Target scores 20-55: real search volume, but major companies do not dominate
- Scores below 20: too niche, not enough volume to matter
- Scores above 55: you are competing with Duolingo, Headspace, and Calm
- Source: TrySonar Apple Search Ads guide (2026), "70% of App Store visitors use search to find apps"

# Screenshots that convert

---

## The first-three rule

- On iPhone in portrait search results, only 3 screenshots are visible before the user must tap "See all"
- Those three screenshots do the same job as a landing page hero section
- Frames 1-3 must convey: what the app does, for whom, and why it is better
- Source: AppFollow ASO Title Playbook (2026), the first-three rule explained with competitive analysis methodology

---

## Benefit framing vs feature labels

<!-- _class: two-col -->

**Feature label (weak)**

- "Dark mode support"
- "Multi-language"
- "Offline mode"
- "Push notifications"

**Benefit framing (strong)**

- "Works the way your eyes do at 11pm"
- "Read in your language"
- "Works on the plane, no WiFi needed"
- "Never miss the moment to practice"

---

## Screenshot hierarchy for a subscription app

1. **Hero:** one core promise, your best visual, benefit caption
2. **Aha moment:** show the moment the user gets the value they paid for
3. **Social proof or stats:** user count, rating, or a one-line review
4. Frames 4-10: feature details for users who are already interested
- Rule: if you remove the caption, the screenshot should still communicate something

# Featuring and localization

---

## What indie apps can actually control for featuring

- Apple features apps that use new platform APIs (WidgetKit, Live Activities, SharePlay) in the release they ship
- Google Play features well-designed apps that follow Material You guidelines
- Tony Dinh (BlackMagic, Wins, XWord) pattern: ship the new API feature fast, email the editorial team with a story about the app
- What you cannot control: whether the editorial team picks your app
- What you can control: the quality of the listing they review when they do

---

## Localization: the highest-leverage ASO action

- A single new language unlocks a new search index entirely
- Title, subtitle, and keywords are localized separately, you can optimize per market
- Start with one market where your app already has organic installs
- A localized title with local keywords outperforms an English title even in non-English markets where users are bilingual
- Source: RevenueCat ASO guide (2022), localization section

# Apple Search Ads for indie budgets

---

## ASA campaign structure for a solo founder

- **Brand campaign:** your own app name as an exact-match keyword, protect it, costs almost nothing
- **Competitor campaign:** exact-match on direct competitor names (expensive, use carefully)
- **Discovery campaign:** search match turned on, let Apple find new keywords, harvest what converts
- Source: TrySonar Apple Search Ads guide (2026)

---

## The $5-50/day framework

- Days 1-7: $5/day discovery campaign. Let the algorithm show your ad for broad relevant terms.
- Review the search terms report. Promote high-CPI/high-converting terms to exact match. Negative-match irrelevant terms.
- Weeks 2-4: raise to $20-50/day on exact-match campaigns for your top 5 keywords.
- After 4-6 weeks: pause. Measure organic keyword rank lift. The ASA spend builds organic momentum.
- Source: TrySonar Apple Search Ads guide (2026), the cyclical strategy

---

## Measuring ASA: CPI vs revenue per install

- Vanity metric: installs from ads
- Actionable metric: revenue per install (RPI) for the ASA cohort vs organic cohort
- If ASA CPI is $2.00 and your trial-to-paid rate is 10% and your price is $29.99/year: RPI = $3.00. Profitable.
- If trial-to-paid is 2%: RPI = $0.60. Not profitable. Fix the paywall before scaling ASA.
- Source: TrySonar Apple Search Ads guide (2026); RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025 (trial-to-paid benchmarks)

---

## Take-aways

1. The app name carries the most ranking weight: spend your 30 characters on brand + primary keyword.
2. The first three screenshots function as a landing page hero, write benefit captions, not feature labels.
3. Featuring is not a strategy; it is a reward for good execution on new platform APIs.
4. Localization is the highest ASO leverage available to a one-person team.
5. ASA works best when your paywall converts, validate conversion before scaling ad spend.
