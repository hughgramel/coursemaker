# The $5k MRR gate

---

## Almost never before $5k MRR

Why paid is premature at $0-$5k MRR:

- You don't have a stable LTV number yet: without 6+ months of cohort data, your CAC ceiling is a guess
- Paid amplifies a leaky funnel: you pay to fill a bucket that has holes
- A paid channel needs a creative iteration loop: $200 per test, five variants minimum. That's $1,000 before you have a signal.
- Most founders at this stage need channel learning, not channel scaling

Exception: a narrow $50-$100 test to validate a message is a research tool, not an acquisition program

---

## The CAC ceiling: the one number that unlocks paid

From the First Round Review consumer-acquisition playbook (Hockenmaier & Rachitsky, 2019):

```
CAC ceiling = LTV / target LTV:CAC ratio

At 3:1 ratio:
  LTV = $120 (12 months × $10/mo)  →  CAC ceiling = $40
  LTV = $300 (12 months × $25/mo)  →  CAC ceiling = $100
  LTV = $600 (6 months × $100/mo)  →  CAC ceiling = $200
```

If your blended CAC from organic channels is already above your ceiling, paid makes things worse, not better

---

## What LTV:CAC ratio to use

Paddle / ProfitWell benchmark (2022) across 30,000+ subscription companies:

- **3:1** is the minimum for a sustainable paid channel
- **5:1** is healthy: leaves margin for creative tests and channel experiments
- **Below 2:1**: you are buying growth at a loss; fix retention or price first
- LTV here means net LTV after refunds, payment fees, and churn. Not gross revenue.

Do not use your best cohort's LTV. Use your median cohort.

# Meta and Google fundamentals

---

## Bid types: what each one optimizes for

Three bid types you will see on both platforms:

- **CPM (cost per thousand impressions):** You pay for reach. Use for brand and retargeting when you control targeting tightly.
- **CPC (cost per click):** You pay for traffic. Use when your landing page is the qualification step and click intent is high.
- **CPA / target ROAS (cost per acquisition / return on ad spend):** You pay for outcomes. Requires 50+ conversion events per week to train the algorithm. Use only when you have sufficient volume.

Start with CPC for a new product at low spend. CPA requires data you don't have yet.

---

## Conversion API: why it matters now

Browser-side pixels have degraded since iOS 14.5 (2021) and GDPR:

- 20-40% of conversions go unattributed if you rely on browser pixel alone
- The conversion API (Meta) or enhanced conversions (Google) sends server-side events directly from your backend, bypassing ad blockers and browser restrictions
- You match server events to user identities via hashed email and phone

Implementation: send a `Purchase` or `CompleteRegistration` event from your Stripe webhook handler, not from the browser

---

## Attribution windows: read this before reporting

- **1-day click / 1-day view:** Tight. Catches only high-intent conversions. Best for evaluating creative quality.
- **7-day click / 1-day view (Meta default):** Standard. Catches research cycles for sub-$50 products.
- **28-day click (Google):** Broad. Can overcount, especially for subscription sign-ups where the first paid month is delayed.

Rule: pick one window before you launch a test and do not change it mid-test. Changing attribution windows makes week-over-week comparison meaningless.

# Creative as the variable

---

## Creative is 80% of paid performance

First Round Review (Hockenmaier & Rachitsky, 2019) on what drives paid results:

- Audience targeting is increasingly automated: Meta and Google are good at finding buyers
- The algorithm optimizes toward your conversion event, but only if the creative qualifies the right person
- A bad creative sent to the right audience converts poorly; a good creative to a rough audience still converts
- Most founders over-invest in targeting and under-invest in creative

Write the creative from customer language, not founder language

---

## The five-variant rule

How to run a minimal paid test:

1. Write five ad creative variants from five different customer pain statements
2. Use the same landing page, same audience, same budget split ($10-$20 per variant)
3. Run for 4-7 days: enough impressions for a directional signal, not statistical certainty
4. Kill the bottom three. Double the budget of the top one. Iterate.
5. Never change targeting and creative in the same test: you won't know which variable drove the result

Total test budget: $50-$200 for a directional signal

---

## Creative that comes from customer language

Tony Dinh on writing from customer language (Indie Hackers Podcast #260, 2022):

- Pull the exact phrases your users use in support tickets, reviews, and DMs
- An ad that says what a user already thinks sounds like truth, not advertising
- "Twitter lets me have a direct line to my users before, during, and after launch. That's worth more than any ad budget."

Your week 7 customer hour: talk to two power users and write five ad headlines directly from what they say

---

## The small-spend, fast-iteration loop

Lenny Rachitsky on Booking.com's paid pivot (2022): they did not scale spend until they had a creative that was demonstrably profitable at small budgets

Adapted for solo founders at $5k-$10k MRR:

```
Week 1: $100 test, 5 creatives, one landing page
Week 2: Kill 3, iterate 2, test a new landing page variant
Week 3: If top creative CAC < ceiling: scale 2x
Week 4: If CAC still holds: scale 2x again
If CAC breaks: stop, diagnose creative or landing page, don't increase targeting budget
```

---

## Take-aways

1. Paid acquisition is almost never the right first move before $5k MRR: it amplifies a leaky funnel.
2. Your CAC ceiling is LTV divided by your target LTV:CAC ratio. Compute it before any spend.
3. Conversion API (server-side events) is required for accurate attribution in a post-iOS 14 world.
4. Creative is the primary variable in paid performance: write it from customer language, not founder language.
5. The test unit is five creative variants at $50-$200 total; decisions come from week-over-week creative comparison, not audience tuning.
