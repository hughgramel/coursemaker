# AARRR mapped to LangoBee

---

## Five stages, LangoBee edition

Dave McClure's AARRR framework (2007) names five conversion stages. Every product maps its own events to them.

| Stage | LangoBee event | Status |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| Acquisition | Pageview on langobee.com | Firing |
| Activation | First reading_event (kind = 'page_read') | Firing |
| Retention | reading_event on day 7+ after signup | Measurable |
| Revenue | trial_started | Zero events ever |
| Referral | Share action on shorts feed | 1 event in 30 days |

Revenue stage has zero events. The paywall is wired; no human has entered it.

(McClure, 2007)

---

## The acquisition picture

30-day acquisition attribution (direct + identified channels):

- **Direct / Discord:** 174 people. This is the founder manually recruiting from a ~30-person Discord.
- **google.com:** 21 people
- **t.co (Twitter):** 5 people

No repeatable paid channel exists. The only proven acquisition channel is founder-driven hand-recruitment. At 4 WAU and $0 MRR, this is the correct channel: manual recruiting surfaces real users cheaply before any channel investment.

(Cheung, 2019: start with active users before investing in acquisition)

---

## The real numbers: June 2026 snapshot

De-alted (founder, alt, and bot accounts removed):

- **Real profiles:** approximately 50 humans out of 103 registered
- **WAU:** 4 (last 7 days, authenticated, de-alted)
- **MAU:** 31 (last 30 days, authenticated, de-alted)
- **MRR:** $0. Zero trial_started events ever.

The raw dashboard shows 103 profiles. That number feels like progress. The de-alted number is the one that determines whether acquisition spend would have a positive return.

4 WAU is the honest baseline.

# Cohort anatomy

---

## Three cohort weeks, three stories

| Cohort week | Signups | Activated | Returned |
|-------------|---------|-----------|----------|
| May 18 | 15 | 12 (80%) | 6 (40%) |
| May 25 | 19 | 7 (37%) | 3 (16%) |
| June 1 | 52 | 1 (2%) | unknown |

Three completely different profiles in three consecutive weeks.

May 18: strong activation, reasonable return. These are likely real comprehensible-input learners recruited directly.

May 25: lower activation, lower return. Broader recruiting, lower-intent audience.

June 1: 51 bots. One real human.

---

## What the May 18 cohort tells you

12 of 15 activated. 6 returned. This is the product working for someone.

The users who activated in that cohort are the signal. Look at what they did:

- They are likely from the Refold / Dreaming Spanish comprehensible-input community
- They used the reader or the YouTube viewer, not the shorts feed
- word_lookup_opened is the top product action at 5,225 events in 30 days: the tap-a-word loop works for this audience

The May 18 cohort is the evidence that the product solves a real problem. It is also the reason not to pivot to a completely different thesis in week 2.

---

## What the June 1 cohort tells you

52 signups, 1 activation. Do not celebrate this as acquisition traction.

Bot-detection checklist for any cohort week:

1. Activation rate below 5%: strong bot signal
2. Email domains: check for known junk TLDs (a7gi.ru, host315.net)
3. Auth provider: all password, no OAuth: bot signal
4. Time to signup: burst of signups in a short window: automated

The June 1 spike almost certainly came from a credential-stuffing or spam-registration script that found the signup endpoint. It produces no revenue, no retention, no signal. Exclude it and move on.

# Choosing a north-star metric

---

## What a north-star metric must do

Rachitsky's (2021) definition: the single number that best represents the value your product delivers to users. It must be:

- A leading indicator of revenue, not revenue itself
- Something users do (not something you do)
- Measurable with the tools you have today

Six NSM categories (Rachitsky, 2021):

1. Revenue (e.g., MRR): lagging, not the NSM
2. Customer growth (e.g., paid subscribers): lagging
3. Consumption growth (e.g., words read): leading
4. Engagement (e.g., sessions per week): leading
5. Growth efficiency: not applicable at this stage
6. User experience: too broad to track

---

## LangoBee's NSM candidates

Three candidates, each tied to a specific product behavior:

**Candidate A: Words encountered per week (de-alted)**
- What it measures: volume of target-language exposure
- Why it leads revenue: comprehension progress drives retention
- Query: `SELECT count(*) FROM reading_events WHERE kind = 'word_seen'`

**Candidate B: Days with at least one reading session (de-alted, last 28 days)**
- What it measures: habit formation, not just binge sessions
- Why it leads revenue: habit is the retention predictor for subscription products

**Candidate C: word_lookup_opened events per WAU per week**
- What it measures: depth of vocabulary engagement per active user
- Why it leads revenue: users who look up words are actively learning, not browsing

Decide in section which one to instrument as the primary metric for weeks 1-4.

## The $0 MRR problem, stated plainly

Stripe is wired. The paywall exists. The trial flow exists. Zero humans have entered it.

This is not a pricing problem. It is a sequencing problem.

You cannot learn whether $7.99/mo is the right price when no user has reached the trial screen. The funnel breaks before revenue.

The correct diagnosis: activation and retention come before monetization. The reading and session section this week establish the honest baseline. Weeks 3-5 fix activation. Weeks 6-8 address monetization. Spending on acquisition or pricing experiments now is spending ahead of learning.

(McClure, 2007: fix the leakiest stage, not the one you prefer to fix)

---

## Take-aways

1. LangoBee's AARRR funnel has events firing at acquisition, activation, and retention; the revenue stage has zero events ever, which is the primary diagnostic.
2. The June 2026 snapshot is: approximately 50 real users, 4 de-alted WAU, 31 MAU, $0 MRR. These are the honest baseline numbers.
3. Cohort analysis separates three completely different stories: strong May 18 week (real CI learners), declining May 25, bot-dominated June 1.
4. word_lookup_opened at 5,225 events in 30 days is the product working; it is the leading signal to track as a north-star metric candidate.
5. The correct sequence is activation and retention first, monetization after. The funnel breaks before revenue; fixing acquisition or pricing now is out of order.
