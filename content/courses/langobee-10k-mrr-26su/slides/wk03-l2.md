# Time-to-value

---

## The clock starts before you think it does

Time-to-value is the elapsed time from when a user opens your product for the first time to when they experience its core value.

For a language app, the core value is: I understood something in my target language that I couldn't before.

The clock starts at the first page load. It does not start after the user finishes onboarding. It includes:

- The signup form
- Language selection
- Any tutorial screens
- Time waiting for content to load
- Time scanning the UI to find what to do

Every second is an opportunity to lose the user.

---

## What "too long" looks like

LangoBee's June 2026 median session: 34 seconds.

If it takes 45 seconds to reach the first word tap under ideal conditions, then half of all users leave before they ever get there.

Industry benchmarks for time-to-value:

- Consumer apps: under 60 seconds to first value moment
- Language apps: under 90 seconds to first comprehension event
- Games: under 30 seconds to first score or win

The benchmark is not a ceiling. It is a floor. Faster is always better.

(Rachitsky, 2022)

---

## The guided first session

A guided first session is an intentionally constrained first-run experience that removes every decision not needed to reach the aha moment.

Not: a tutorial that teaches features.
Not: a series of permission prompts.
Not: a profile setup wizard.

A guided first session for LangoBee:

1. Signup (or skip) - one screen
2. Spanish selected by default (editable later)
3. Feed opens on the highest-rated beginner video
4. A subtle tap prompt overlays the first word in the first caption
5. User taps the word. Popup opens. Aha.

Total target: under 60 seconds.

(Hulick, 2013)

# Defining activation

---

## Activation is not signup

Activation is the earliest action that predicts long-term retention.

Signup is not activation. Signup means the user gave you an email address. Activation means they experienced the thing you are selling.

For LangoBee, the candidate aha actions:

- Session starts (too early: no value delivered yet)
- First video plays (early: value is possible but not delivered)
- word_lookup_opened (the aha: the user interacted with the learning loop)
- First word marked known (too late: requires multiple sessions)

word_lookup_opened is the right definition: it is the earliest action that requires the full product to work and delivers an immediate experience of the core value.

(Rachitsky, 2022)

---

## Why word_lookup_opened is the right activation event

word_lookup_opened is the top product action in the June 2026 snapshot: 5,225 events in 30 days.

Users who fire this event have:
- Loaded the feed
- Watched at least part of a video with captions
- Seen a word they wanted to understand
- Tapped and received a definition

That is the full comprehensible-input loop. A user who completes that loop once has evidence that the product works for them. A user who does not complete it has no reason to return.

---

## Measuring activation rate

Activation rate = signups who fire word_lookup_opened within 24 hours / total signups (real, bot-excluded)

PostHog query:

```
Funnel:
  Step 1: user signed up
  Step 2: word_lookup_opened
  Conversion window: 24 hours
  Date range: last 30 days
  Exclude: founder account, test emails
```

Benchmarks from Rachitsky and Timen (2022): median 25%, mean 34%. Good: 30%+. Great: 45%+.

Run the query now. Write the number in your founder journal.

## Designing the path backward

Start from the aha moment and remove steps.

word_lookup_opened requires: a video is playing, captions are visible, a word is tappable.

Every step in the onboarding that does not contribute to those three conditions is a candidate for removal or deferral.

Common steps that sound necessary but are not:
- Push notification permission (defer to day 3)
- Profile photo upload (defer to week 1)
- "How did you find us" survey (remove entirely)
- Difficulty calibration quiz (set a default, let the algorithm learn)

Each removed step shrinks time-to-value. Each shrink improves activation rate.

(Hulick, 2013)

---

## What to do with a low activation rate

Rachitsky and Timen (2022) report that a rate below 25% indicates a broken onboarding, not a broken product.

The diagnosis sequence:

1. Below 25%: fix onboarding first. The product may be fine; users are not reaching it.
2. 25-34%: improve the guided first session. Look at what activated users did versus non-activated users in the first 24 hours.
3. Above 34%: activation is acceptable. Retention (day-7 return rate) is now the constraint.

If word_lookup_opened has 5,225 events but most come from a small group of power users (the founder plus 3-4 real users), activation rate is misleading. Segment by cohort week before drawing conclusions.

---

## Take-aways

1. Time-to-value starts at first page load, not after onboarding. Every second between landing and the aha moment is a leak.
2. A guided first session is not a tutorial. It is the shortest possible path to the first experience of core value, with every unnecessary step removed.
3. word_lookup_opened is LangoBee's activation event: the first tap of a word that delivers an immediate experience of the learning loop.
4. The activation rate benchmark is 25% median. Below 25% means fix onboarding before anything else.
5. Design the guided first session backward from the aha moment: remove every step that does not contribute to getting a video playing with tappable captions.
