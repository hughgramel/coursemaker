# Funnels vs loops

---

## A funnel consumes; a loop compounds

A linear acquisition funnel has a top and a bottom. Every user enters from outside, moves through stages, and exits. Growth is proportional to what you pour in at the top.

A growth loop has no bottom. The output of one cycle becomes the input of the next:

- Engagement loop: users who learn share what they learned, which brings new users who learn
- Acquisition loop: new users who see the product share it, which brings more new users
- Monetization loop: revenue funds content that improves the product for paying users

(Balfour, Winters, Kwok, and Chen, 2018)

---

## Why this matters for a solo founder

A funnel requires continuous investment at the top. A loop, once turning, requires none.

At LangoBee's stage:
- Every Discord recruit is a funnel input you manufactured
- Every share of a clip is a loop output that could seed a new input
- 174 direct recruits produced 31 monthly actives: an entirely manufactured funnel

The question for distribution phase: can you engineer one loop that turns without you?

# The candidate loop

---

## The shorts-share-viewer loop

This is LangoBee's one candidate acquisition loop. It has four stages:

1. **Feed impression:** a logged-in user sees a clip in the shorts feed
2. **Completion:** they watch to the end (the quality signal)
3. **Share:** they send the clip to someone outside the app
4. **New viewer signup:** the recipient discovers LangoBee and signs up

The output (a new signup) becomes a new feed impression on the next cycle.

---

## What the June 2026 data says

Measure each stage honestly (June 2026 snapshot):

- Feed impressions (30 days): 408
- Completions: 101 (28% completion rate; 63% skip)
- Shares: 1
- New viewer signups from that share: unknown, likely 0

Share rate: 1/408 = 0.0025. K-factor requires share rate times conversion rate of new viewers. With an unknown second multiplier, K is effectively 0.

This loop is not turning.

---

## The viral coefficient, defined

The K-factor formula:

```
K = (shares per user) x (signups per share)
```

At K = 1, every user brings in one new user. The product maintains size without acquisition spend.
At K less than 1, the loop does not sustain itself. Growth requires outside input.
At K greater than 1, the user base grows exponentially on its own.

LangoBee's K is near zero. One share from 13 real feed users is not a loop; it is noise.

(Chen, 2009)

---

## K less than 1 does not mean the loop is useless

Chen's original framing is often misread. K greater than 1 is not the goal; a working loop is.

A product with K = 0.3 and strong LTV still compounds faster than a funnel-only competitor at the same LTV. Each loop cycle seeds new users at near-zero marginal cost.

The question is not "is K greater than 1?" It is "is K above zero in a measurable way?" For LangoBee today, the honest answer is: we do not know, because the sample is too small to measure.

(Chen, 2009)

# What would change the number

---

## The two multipliers you can control

K has two terms. You can improve either:

**Shares per user:**
- Is the share button visible after completion?
- Does the shared link work without an account?
- Is the shared content interesting enough that the recipient watches?

**Signups per share:**
- Does the share land on a frictionless page?
- Is there a clear reason to create an account vs just watch?

LangoBee's current share link behavior: unknown. This is the first thing to audit.

---

## The one metric to track this week

To know if the loop is turning, you need one number: **share-to-signup rate.**

Define it: of all clips shared from the feed in the last 30 days, what percentage produced at least one new authenticated user within 7 days?

With 1 share in 30 days, you cannot compute this yet. The target for a measurable signal: 20+ shares. That requires the share surface to work and the clip count in feed to grow.

## Loop vs funnel for LangoBee right now

**Funnel (what exists)**

Discord recruit -> signup -> maybe returns
Output: 31 MAUs
Cost: your time, every cycle

**Loop (candidate)**

Feed impression -> completion -> share -> new viewer
Output: unknown, K near 0
Cost: near zero once working

The funnel is the business today. The loop is the thesis for distribution phase. Neither invalidates the other.

## Take-aways

1. A funnel produces growth proportional to input at the top. A loop produces outputs that seed the next cycle, compounding over time.
2. LangoBee's candidate loop is shorts -> share -> new viewer -> shorts. It is not turning: 1 share from 408 impressions in 30 days.
3. The K-factor at LangoBee is effectively zero, not because the loop is broken but because the sample is too small and the share surface is unaudited.
4. K less than 1 does not make a loop worthless. A K of 0.3 with strong LTV still beats a pure funnel at the same LTV.
5. The one metric to instrument: share-to-signup rate. The threshold for a measurable signal: 20 shares.
