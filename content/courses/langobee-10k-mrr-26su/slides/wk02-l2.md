# The Ries pivot taxonomy

---

## Seven named pivot types

Eric Ries (2011) named seven pivot types that cover almost every strategic fork a pre-PMF startup faces:

1. **Zoom-in:** a feature becomes the whole product
2. **Zoom-out:** the current product becomes a feature in a larger one
3. **Customer segment:** same problem, different customer
4. **Customer need:** same customer, different problem
5. **Platform:** from app to platform, or vice versa
6. **Business architecture:** high-margin low-volume vs low-margin high-volume
7. **Technology:** same positioning, different implementation

The taxonomy matters because each type has a different evidence requirement and a different build cost.

(Ries, 2011)

---

## The zoom-in pivot in detail

A zoom-in pivot happens when one feature of an existing product is doing all the work and the rest is noise.

The feature-as-product becomes the new thesis. The remaining features either migrate to support it or are cut.

Evidence profile: the feature's engagement metrics (completion rate, return rate, share rate) exceed every other surface in the product by a meaningful margin.

(Ries, 2011; First Round Review, 2023)

---

## The shorts feed as a zoom-in candidate

LangoBee's full product: reader, YouTube video viewer, and a short-form vertical feed.

The shorts feed in June 2026: 408 impressions, 101 completions, 13 real users, 1 share, 28% completion rate, 63% skip rate.

The question a zoom-in pivot asks: if you removed every other surface and shipped only the shorts feed, would the 13 users who came back be the seed of a product people love?

The answer is not yet known. The thesis has not been written. The time box has not been set.

(June 2026 snapshot)

# Time-boxed experiments

---

## What makes an experiment time-boxed

A time-boxed experiment has four required fields:

1. **Hypothesis:** a falsifiable claim about user behavior.
2. **Metric:** the one number that will confirm or deny the hypothesis.
3. **Kill threshold:** the number below which you stop and pivot.
4. **Decision date:** the calendar date at which you read the result and decide.

Without all four, the experiment cannot be killed. It runs forever, consuming runway.

(Duke, 2022; Ries, 2011)

---

## The pre-mortem for a fork decision

Annie Duke (2022) introduces the pre-mortem as the mechanism that makes kill criteria stick. Before the experiment starts:

1. Imagine it is the decision date. The metric missed the kill threshold.
2. Write down every story you would tell yourself for why the result does not count.
3. Write a counter-story for each one.
4. Set the kill threshold at a level you would accept even given the best counter-story.

For LangoBee's fork: the pre-mortem question is "What would I tell myself if 0 trials start by July 15?" Write those stories before the experiment runs, not after.

(Duke, 2022)

---

## The four-lever framework

First Round Review (2023) documents pre-PMF stagnation across Vanta, Plaid, Clay, and Lattice using a four-lever model:

- **Problem:** is the problem urgent and real for this person?
- **Persona:** is this the right customer for this problem?
- **Promise:** does the product promise match what they care about?
- **Product:** does the product deliver on the promise?

The finding across all four companies: recovery required a 200% adjustment on one lever, not incremental improvement across all four. Spreading effort across levers produces local optima, not escapes from stagnation.

(First Round Review, 2023)

---

## Applying the four levers to LangoBee's fork

<!-- _class: two-col -->

**Library thesis**

Problem: comprehensible-input learners lack a unified premium content surface. Persona: self-directed adult Spanish learners. Promise: the best native-content library at $7.99/mo. Product lever: 17,440 texts shipped. Gap: promise-to-product conversion (no trials started).

**Shorts thesis**

Problem: comprehensible-input learners want short discovery without commitment. Persona: casual social-media learners. Promise: the most shareable Spanish learning feed. Product lever: 28% completion, 1 share. Gap: persona and promise not yet validated.

Each thesis is weak on a different lever. One experiment cannot test both.

# The fork decision memo

---

## Required fields in the memo

A fork decision memo is not a brainstorm or a preference statement. It is a structured document with required fields:

1. **The two hypotheses,** stated falsifiably (not "we will explore").
2. **The metric each hypothesis is judged on,** named and defined.
3. **The kill threshold for each,** stated as a number and a condition.
4. **The time box,** a specific calendar range.
5. **The decision date,** a specific calendar date.
6. **Who decides,** even if it is the founder alone.

## Take-aways

1. Ries named seven pivot types. Naming the type forces clarity about what is actually changing: the product, the customer, the business model, or the problem.
2. A zoom-in pivot treats one feature as the new whole product. It requires evidence that the feature's engagement exceeds the rest of the product.
3. Every time-boxed experiment needs a hypothesis, a metric, a kill threshold, and a decision date. Missing any one of these turns the experiment into open-ended building.
4. The four-lever framework picks the single most misaligned lever. LangoBee's two fork directions are each weak on different levers.
5. The fork decision memo is the section deliverable: two falsifiable hypotheses, explicit kill thresholds, a time box, and a decision date.
